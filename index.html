Research identity

I am an interdisciplinary researcher integrating qualitative inquiry, behavioral science, human-computer interaction, health informatics, and artificial intelligence to understand people first—and then design technologies that support lasting health.

Vision

Supporting lifelong health through education, behavior change, and intelligent human-centered technologies.

Health is shaped by everyday decisions sustained over time. Whether someone is preventing illness, adopting healthier habits, recovering from surgery, or managing a chronic condition, long-term success depends on continued engagement—not a single intervention.

My goal is to build trustworthy digital health ecosystems that educate, motivate, adapt, and collaborate with individuals. These systems should help people understand their own data, make sense of their progress, and receive the right support at the right time.

This journey may begin with a healthy person building preventive habits, or it may provide scaffolding for a patient navigating treatment, rehabilitation, and long-term care.

Research approach

People first. Technology second.

I combine qualitative, quantitative, human-centered, and computational methods to move from understanding lived experience to designing and evaluating meaningful interventions.

01

Discover

Explore experiences, motivations, barriers, and contexts through interviews, focus groups, surveys, and stakeholder collaboration.

02

Develop theory

Use qualitative analysis and mixed methods to identify patterns, explain behavior, and build actionable conceptual models.

03

Design

Create human-centered digital health tools, mobile applications, wearables, and AI-supported interventions.

04

Evaluate

Assess usability, engagement, adherence, outcomes, equity, and real-world implementation.
