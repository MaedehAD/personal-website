I am an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of health informatics, human-computer interaction, information behavior, and education.

My work focuses on supporting lifelong health through education, behavior change, and intelligent human-centered technologies. I study how people learn, adopt, and sustain healthy behaviors, and how technology can support them across prevention, rehabilitation, care management, and healthy aging.

My research has evolved from studying eHealth literacy, information overload, and information avoidance to designing and evaluating mobile and wearable health technologies and, more recently, to explainable AI and federated learning in healthcare.

The main thread across my work is that how technology can support and facilitate well-being based on people's value, motivations, barriers, experiences, and contexts. My methodological background includes qualitative research, theory development, survey research, usability evaluation, mixed methods, and machine learning.

I currently teach and collaborate across Florida State University, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and the University of Pittsburgh.
