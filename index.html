I am Maedeh Agharazi (Agharazidermani), an Adjunct Professor at the Florida State University (FSU) School of Information and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign School of Information Sciences. I am also a collaborating faculty member with the HEX AI Lab at the University of Pittsburgh. Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Associate in the Department of Computer & Information Science & Engineering at the University of Florida (UF) from 2022 to 2024.

I received my PhD in Information from FSU. I also hold two master’s degrees: a master’s degree in Multimedia, with a specialization in e-learning technologies, from Multimedia University (MMU), and a master’s degree in Sociocultural and International Development Education Studies (SIDES) from FSU. My bachelor’s degree is in Information Systems Engineering. This interdisciplinary background informs my research at the intersection of human-computer interaction, education, and health informatics.

Through my work, I focus on supporting lifelong health through education, behavior change, and human-centered technologies. My research has evolved from studying eHealth literacy, information overload, and information avoidance to designing and evaluating mobile and wearable health technologies and, more recently, adaptive AI-powered home rehabilitation, explainable AI, and federated learning in healthcare. My work investigates how adaptive digital technologies can improve health literacy, strengthen self-efficacy, support healthy behavior change, and sustain engagement with health and rehabilitation activities.

My research has also included collaborative physical-activity technologies, wearable health applications, and patient engagement. My methodological background includes qualitative research, theory development, survey research, usability evaluation, mixed methods, and machine learning. My publications have appeared in venues such as npj Health Systems, JMIR Human Factors, The Gerontologist, and MobileHCI, where my collaborative cycling research received a Best Late-Breaking Work Award.
