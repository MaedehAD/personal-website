About

Understanding people before designing technology

I am an interdisciplinary researcher whose work integrates qualitative inquiry, behavioral science, human-computer interaction, health informatics, and artificial intelligence. My research seeks to understand how people learn, adopt, and sustain healthy behaviors across prevention, rehabilitation, chronic care, and healthy aging.

My work has evolved from studying health information behavior and eHealth literacy to designing and evaluating mobile and wearable technologies, and more recently to applying explainable AI and federated learning in healthcare. Across these areas, one principle remains constant: technology should adapt to people—not the other way around.

Vision, approach, and methods

Qualitative inquiry

Interviews, focus groups, thematic analysis, theory development, NVivo, and research on motivations, barriers, and lived experience.

Quantitative research

Survey design, psychometrics, structural equation modeling, statistical analysis, and program evaluation.

Human-centered design

Usability studies, mobile and wearable technology evaluation, co-design, and iterative collaboration with stakeholders.

Intelligent health systems

Predictive modeling, explainable AI, federated learning, digital rehabilitation, and privacy-aware healthcare analytics.
