Maedeh Agharazi (Agharazidermani) is an Adjunct Professor at the Florida State University School of Information and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign School of Information Sciences. She is also collaborating faculty with the HEX AI Lab at the University of Pittsburgh and a research collaborator in the Department of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation at the University of Florida. Previously, she was a Postdoctoral Associate in the Department of Computer & Information Science & Engineering at the University of Florida from 2022 to 2024. She received her PhD in Information from Florida State University in 2021. Agharazi’s interdisciplinary research lies at the intersection of health informatics, human-computer interaction, behavioral science, and human-centered artificial intelligence. Her work investigates how adaptive digital technologies can improve health literacy, strengthen self-efficacy, support healthy behavior change, and sustain engagement with health and rehabilitation activities. Her research has included AI-assisted home rehabilitation, collaborative physical-activity technologies, wearable health applications, patient engagement, explainable AI, and federated learning in healthcare. Her publications have appeared in venues such as npj Health Systems, JMIR Human Factors, The Gerontologist, and MobileHCI, where her collaborative cycling research received a Best Late-Breaking Work Award. I am an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of health informatics, human-computer interaction, information behavior, and education.

My work focuses on supporting lifelong health through education, behavior change, and intelligent human-centered technologies. I study how people learn, adopt, and sustain healthy behaviors, and how technology can support them across prevention, rehabilitation, care management, and healthy aging.

My research has evolved from studying eHealth literacy, information overload, and information avoidance to designing and evaluating mobile and wearable health technologies and, more recently, to explainable AI and federated learning in healthcare.

The main thread across my work is that how technology can support and facilitate well-being based on people's value, motivations, barriers, experiences, and contexts. My methodological background includes qualitative research, theory development, survey research, usability evaluation, mixed methods, and machine learning.

I currently teach and collaborate across Florida State University, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and the University of Pittsburgh.
