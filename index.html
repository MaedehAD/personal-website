I am an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of health informatics, human-computer interaction, behavioral science, qualitative inquiry, and artificial intelligence.

My work focuses on supporting lifelong health through education, behavior change, and intelligent human-centered technologies. I study how people learn, adopt, and sustain healthy behaviors, and how technology can support them across prevention, rehabilitation, chronic care, and healthy aging.

My research has evolved from studying eHealth literacy, information overload, and information avoidance to designing and evaluating mobile and wearable health technologies and, more recently, to explainable AI and federated learning in healthcare.

A consistent theme across my work is that technology should begin with an understanding of people—their motivations, barriers, experiences, and contexts. My methodological background includes qualitative research, theory development, survey research, usability evaluation, mixed methods, and machine learning.

I currently teach and collaborate across Florida State University, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, the University of Pittsburgh, and the University of Florida.
