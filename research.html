Research

Research vision

Every healthy life is a journey. My research explores how education, behavior change, and intelligent human-centered technologies can help people stay on that path—from prevention to rehabilitation and lifelong wellness.

Research pillars

Educate
Support health literacy and help people understand preventive behaviors, healthy habits, and why persistence matters.
Engage
Use behavioral science and change-management strategies to help people adopt and sustain meaningful health behaviors.
Understand
Collect meaningful data and help individuals interpret their own health information through explainable, human-centered systems.
Sustain
Create adaptive support for healthy individuals and scaffolding for patients throughout treatment, rehabilitation, and long-term care.

Research approach

I approach research as an iterative process that starts with people rather than technology.

Understand people → Develop theory → Design → Evaluate → Improve

Methodological expertise

Qualitative inquiry: interviews, focus groups, thematic analysis, theory development, NVivo, and research on motivations, barriers, and lived experience.

Quantitative research: survey design, psychometrics, structural equation modeling, statistical analysis, and program evaluation.

Human-centered design: usability studies, mobile and wearable technology evaluation, co-design, and iterative collaboration with developers and stakeholders.

Intelligent health systems: predictive modeling, explainable AI, federated learning, digital rehabilitation, and privacy-aware healthcare analytics.

Selected qualitative research

African American women in computing. I analyzed interview data in NVivo and contributed to theory development examining familial influences and persistence in the computing pipeline.

Motivation to engage in aging research. As part of the NIH-funded Adherence Promotion with Person-centered Technology (APPT) project, I analyzed qualitative survey and focus-group data and contributed to theory development around older adults’ motivations to participate in research.
